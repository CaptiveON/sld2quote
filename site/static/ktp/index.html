The KTP

What it asks for, how I would run it, and what the miniature already shows

The partnership in its own words, a 36-month plan in work packages with what this miniature already demonstrates for each, how a KTP works, the risks, and the road map from each part of the miniature to its real version.

1. What the KTP asks for

The project, in the advert's words

"This KTP will develop a secure, explainable AI system that automatically reads, interprets and converts SLDs and specifications into accurate Bills of Materials and costed quotations, automating the SLD-to-quotation process. The innovation lies in combining computer vision, natural language processing and large language models into a single multimodal reasoning engine that can understand drawings and text together, supported by explainability to show how engineering decisions were made and secure by design deployment appropriate for regulated sectors." [2]

The purpose, in the job description's words

"By embedding AI technologies throughout the estimation workflow, R&B expects to significantly improve operational efficiency, increase capacity and establish a competitive advantage within the electrical engineering sector. The project will also create new organisational capabilities in artificial intelligence, data engineering, safe AI and digital automation." [1]

The six objectives [1]

  1. "Gain a detailed understanding of R&B Switchgear's products, systems, processes and estimation workflows."
  2. "Develop and implement AI solutions capable of analysing complex engineering information across multimodal sources."
  3. "Develop computer vision, natural language processing and large language model applications to streamline time-intensive engineering and operational activities."
  4. "Create secure, safe and auditable AI systems suitable for use in engineering and manufacturing environments."
  5. "Develop and implement data engineering, integration and deployment frameworks to support long-term use of AI technologies."
  6. "Develop and embed AI, machine learning and digital innovation capabilities within R&B Switchgear through training, documentation and knowledge transfer activities."

3. How a KTP works

A Knowledge Transfer Partnership is a three-way partnership between a business, an academic partner (the knowledge base) and a graduate, the Associate [4]. For this post the Associate is employed by the University of Salford and based within R&B's operations at Switchgear House in Heywood, travelling regularly to the university [1]. The company provides day-to-day supervision; the university provides at least half a day a week of academic supervision; a monthly face-to-face project meeting of the Associate, the academic supervisor and the business is mandatory; a Local Management Committee, chaired by the business's senior employee and attended by the Associate, oversees progress; and the university commits to at least two publications and a case study at the end [3]. Associates spend 10% of their time on their own development and are mentored by a Knowledge Transfer Adviser [5], [6]. The point of the arrangement is embedding: the capability has to stay in the company when the project ends, which is why the plan ends with people, procedures and rules the company owns, not with a model.

The road map

Each part of this miniature replaced by its real version

In the order the evidence suggests. It is presented as a road map, not a finished solution: no phase promises a number, each says what would be measured.

  1. Data and labelling with the estimators

    Goal
    A labelled set of the company's own past enquiries: a box and a type on every symbol, each rating linked to its device, the clauses, and the estimator's final bill of materials as the answer key; a symbol vocabulary agreed with the estimators first.
    What the demo does
    Draws its own synthetic drawings with answer keys, audits them like code, and keeps a small public-domain set of real drawings for an honest, separate look.
    What the KTP does differently
    Real drawings from real consultants, labelled by the people who will use the tool, with every label checked against the pixels it describes and the input class (native CAD, vector PDF, scan) recorded.
    Risk
    Too few or too uniform drawings; synthetic drawings seeded from real topologies fill the gap, and test-set sizes are set from the rarest symbol, not from convenience.

  2. The detector trained on real drawings

    Goal
    A symbol detector that spans every consultant's style, with its threshold chosen on whole drawings and a per-type scorecard at drawing level.
    What the demo does
    An RT-DETR detector trained on synthetic tiles, merged across tiles with duplicates made impossible, scored per type on clean, scan-like and unseen-style drawings.
    What the KTP does differently
    Trains on the labelled real sheets, adds a connectivity trace so the output is a circuit rather than a list of boxes, and counts false boxes on blank paper as their own number.
    Risk
    A new consultant's style arriving unseen, and rare symbols staying weak as styles widen; met by a validation set of unseen styles and a per-type recall curve as the early warning.

  3. The learned symbol-to-label link

    Goal
    A model that assigns each piece of text to the device it belongs to and joins device schedules by tag, trained on the estimators' corrections.
    What the demo does
    Rules: symbols on one vertical line form a way, the text lines stacked beside or below it form its label block, the block goes to the nearest way (preferring its own side) and each line is routed to a device by what it says. They were written against this generator's drawing conventions and tuned on the sets they are scored on, so their link numbers in the scorecards are an upper bound; the held-out layouts hold styles out from the detector, not from the rule's author.
    What the KTP does differently
    Replaces the rules with a learned link trained on the estimators' corrections and measured with its own precision and recall apart from symbol finding and text reading, on drawings from consultants the rule's author never saw.
    Risk
    Without corrections flowing from the workbench there is nothing to train on, which is why the workbench comes early.

  4. The specification reader with clause citations

    Goal
    Every requirement extracted into a fixed schema with its clause as evidence, unusual clauses flagged by retrieval from past tenders, and a draft compliance statement.
    What the demo does
    Splits the clauses and reads form, fault level, IP, poles and busbar material by rules, then raises every disagreement with the drawing as a question.
    What the KTP does differently
    A language model inside the company's boundary that must cite the clause or abstain, with the sector overlays the company meets and a measured hallucination rate.
    Risk
    A confident answer without evidence, and prompt injection through document text; clauses are treated as data, never as instructions.

  5. The rules and the catalogue, owned by the estimators

    Goal
    The company's design rules written down as rules the estimators can read, test and change, with supplier price lists dated and versioned; the model drafts, it never chooses a fault rating.
    What the demo does
    Deterministic rules over an invented catalogue, every line carrying its rule and evidence, nothing outside the fault level ever selected silently.
    What the KTP does differently
    The licensed system's verified limits, the company's labour norms per functional unit, and a test for every rule that reproduces past quotations within an agreed band.
    Risk
    Rules that live in people's heads and differ between estimators; written down, they become the company's property and survive the project.

  6. The review workbench and the human in the loop

    Goal
    The estimator's screen: drawing and schedule side by side, doubtful lines first, correction in place, nothing sent to a customer without acceptance; every correction logged as audit trail and training data.
    What the demo does
    The Try it page: the boxes on the sheet, the tables beside them, the questions listed, the files to download.
    What the KTP does differently
    Integration with the estimating sheets and the tools already in use, automatic acceptance only after thresholds are calibrated per field on real enquiries.
    Risk
    A tool slower than the sheet it replaces, or a draft treated as the answer; confidence and evidence stay on every line.

  7. Evaluation gates and the regression set

    Goal
    A golden set of real enquiries with estimator-verified bills of materials; symbols, lines and price measured per input class and sector; no change ships that lowers a gated number.
    What the demo does
    Scores every drawing against its answer key at three levels and keeps every run's numbers in the evaluation files this site reads.
    What the KTP does differently
    Real answer keys, acceptance bands agreed per field, a regression run on every model, prompt or rule change, and a quarterly report to the project's management committee.
    Risk
    Small test sets making pass or fail a coin toss, and thresholds chosen on the wrong unit; every threshold is chosen on whole enquiries, on validation data that is never the test data.

  8. Secure on-premise deployment and explainability

    Goal
    A service inside the company's existing security boundary, open-weight models for anything that sees customer drawings, versioned models and prompts, logging and drift monitoring, a threat model following the UK code of practice for the cyber security of AI [35] and the NCSC guidelines [36], with the OWASP list of language-model risks as the test plan [37].
    What the demo does
    Runs on a small cloud instance with an invented catalogue; every value on this site points to its box, clause, rule or price row.
    What the KTP does differently
    Customer drawings never leave the building unless a contract and a data-protection assessment allow it; a security review before go-live and after each model change; an explanation test with the estimators.
    Risk
    Data residency with API models, and a model change that silently shifts behaviour; re-tested after every change.

  9. Roll-out, and measuring hours saved and tender win rate

    Goal
    Shadow mode first, then assisted mode one sector at a time; estimators trained as reviewers and labellers; the capability written into the quality procedures so it outlives the project.
    What the demo does
    Shows the shape of the gain on synthetic drawings and records every problem met in plain language.
    What the KTP does differently
    A baseline measured before anything changes (time per enquiry, errors found later, tenders won), then hours per quotation, re-work on revisions and win rate tracked over time with their caveats.
    Risk
    Measuring after without a before, and attributing a change in win rate to the tool alone.
