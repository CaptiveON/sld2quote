The miniature now, and the system after the KTP

Two diagrams of the same shape, each with its text version, then the parts compared row by row and a plain statement of what the miniature proves and does not prove.

sld2quote has a part for every step of the system the KTP will build: something that makes training data, a symbol finder, a text reader, the link between symbols and their labels, a specification check, pricing rules, outputs an estimator can check, a scorecard, a site and a deployment. Each part is small, and each part is measured. This page shows the parts and how they connect today, the same picture after a 36-month KTP, a row-by-row comparison, and what the miniature proves and does not prove.

How to read the diagrams: arrows follow the data from left to right and top to bottom; a dashed arrow is feedback (a measurement or a correction that changes an earlier part); a dotted arrow is hosting; a dashed box is a part that exists only in the plan. After the KTP

The system after the KTP

The same chain, rebuilt on the company's own enquiries, with one big change: the loop. Today the right answers come from my generator. After the KTP they come from the estimators' corrections in a review workbench, so the system learns from the people who use it, and every correction is also the audit trail. The months refer to the plan on The KTP page; it is my proposal, not an agreed workplan.

What changes, in one paragraph

Every part keeps its interface, so each can be replaced and measured on its own: the detector is retrained, the text comes from the file when it can, the link is learnt, the specification is read by a language model that must cite its clause, and the rules and prices become the company's own, versioned and owned by its engineers. A language model never chooses a rating or a price: published work on engineering drawings shows even a fine-tuned vision-language model inventing fields [19], so models draft and point to their evidence, and the rules and the estimator decide.

Honestly

Why this is a partial solution, and what it proves

What it is not

  • It has never read one of the company's drawings. Its scores are on drawings from its own generator, so they should be read as an upper bound.
  • Its rules were tuned on the sets they are scored on. The check on fresh drawings feeds the answer key's own symbols and texts to the rules; it tests the rules, not the whole reader.
  • The real public-domain drawings are not scored yet. They use American symbols that the detector has never seen.
  • Its prices are invented and its specification reader knows only the generator's clauses. It reads images, not CAD files. It has no workbench, no corrections loop, no integration and no company security boundary.

What it proves

  • The whole chain works end to end. From a drawing to a priced bill of materials with the evidence on every line, and anyone with a browser can run it.
  • Every stage can be measured on its own. A failure can be traced to the stage that caused it, which is what makes a real system improvable rather than a black box.
  • The hard problems are known before the project starts. No labelled data, rare symbols, unseen styles, the label link, text crossed by lines, tile scores that disagree with drawing scores, a hardware fault, the limits of a free deployment. Each maps to a work package in the plan.
  • I can run a project like this. Plan it in steps with acceptance criteria, take decisions on evidence, record problems as they happen, ship to a fixed date and say plainly what is not done.
