# Real test set: sources and provenance

Nineteen genuine one-line (single-line) diagrams taken from eight documents published by agencies of the United States federal government. They are for scoring the readers on real drawings only, never for training (HANDOFF.md section 2, Data row). Nothing here comes from R&B. The sheets are not labelled yet; labelling is a later task. Per-sheet records with image sizes and hashes are in `manifest.json`; `contact_sheet.png` shows the whole set.

## How the set was assembled

The search started from the federal design manuals and criteria named in the brief (Unified Facilities Criteria on the Whole Building Design Guide, Army and Navy technical and operations manuals, the Department of Veterans Affairs electrical design manual) because these are written to show typical switchboard and switchgear arrangements. Each candidate document was downloaded into its own folder under `raw/`, untouched, with a `note.txt` giving the URL, the final URL after redirects, the retrieval time and a SHA-256 hash. Its text layer was searched for figure captions, credit lines ("courtesy", "reprinted", "copyright", "source") and the rights statement, and the candidate pages were looked at as low-resolution previews. A figure was kept only when (a) the document is a federal work or says it is public property, and the figure itself carries no credit to a company, contractor, standards body or book, and (b) it is a real one-line diagram with device symbols (breakers, transformers, generators, transfer switches), not a block diagram, wiring diagram or photograph. Within a document, near-duplicate topologies were skipped so that no single source dominates. Kept figures were rendered to grey PNG with pypdfium2 at 200 dpi, or at 300 dpi where the figure is small on the page or its symbols are small, cropped to the drawing (captions left out, they are recorded instead), and rotated upright where the drawing is printed sideways. Two archived UFC editions carry a large diagonal CANCELLED stamp added by the archive as a separate object on each page; that object was left out of the render so the drawing is read as published, and this is recorded per sheet. The result mixes clean vector drawings (3), clean raster exports of CAD drawings (7), low-resolution rasters (3) and scanned printed figures (6). All use the US ANSI/IEEE 315 symbol style (arc or square breakers, coil or zig-zag transformers, circled G for generators), not the IEC and UK box styles of the synthetic sheets, and most carry few or no ampere ratings, so scores are expected to be well below the synthetic ones. Two sheets are medium-voltage switchgear or substations rather than low-voltage boards (`tm5-694-p43`, `ufc3-550-03fa-p28`), and the VA and MO-201 sheets show the medium-voltage side together with the low-voltage boards; they are kept because the symbols and layout conventions are the same.

## Exclusions

Figures and documents that were looked at and left out, with the reason:

1. TM 5-811-6 Electric Power Plant Design (Department of the Army, 20 January 1984; `raw/tm5-811-6`). Figures 4-1 to 4-5 are genuine one-line diagrams (station connections, a typical station-service system with 480 V load centres and motor control centres, a synchronising bus) and the manual says it is public property, but each of these figures is credited "Courtesy of Pope, Evans and Robbins (Non-Copyrighted)", an engineering firm. Excluded under the contractor rule despite the "non-copyrighted" note.
2. TM 5-693: Figures 4-1, 4-2 and 4-4 are credited to Liebert, Exide and HITEC Power Protection (equipment pictures, not one-lines). Figures 2-1 to 2-4, 2-22 to 2-31 and 3-5 are block diagrams of rectifiers, inverters and static switches with few or no device symbols. Figure 2-5 is a one-line of a UPS maintenance bypass but is embedded as a 412 by 207 pixel image at 72 dpi with illegible text.
3. TM 5-694: Figure 3-1 is credited to Liebert. Figures 4-2 to 4-7, 5-2, 5-3, 6-2 and 6-3 are block, functional and wiring diagrams.
4. UFC 3-540-01: Figure B-10 is a control-system block diagram; B-11 and B-12 are three-line ATS wiring diagrams. B-2, B-3, B-5, B-7 and B-8 are eligible but repeat the topologies already taken; they can be added if more sheets are wanted.
5. UFC 3-520-01 (2002): Figure 2-1 is a screenshot of a commercial power-analysis program's interface (third-party software), excluded. Figure 11-1 is pictorial. Figures 3-1 and B-1 to B-3 are eligible but simple (3-1 is a subset of 3-2; B-1 to B-3 are short-circuit result sketches).
6. UFC 3-550-03FA (TM 5-811-1 body): Figure 2-1 is credited to McGraw-Hill's Standard Handbook for Electrical Engineers, excluded (and not a one-line). Figure 1-1 (credited to USACE, medium-voltage primary arrangements) is eligible but not taken. Figures 4-5 and 4-6 are plans and elevations.
7. NAVFAC MO-201: Figures 3-11 to 3-19, 4-5 and 4-6 are company photographs (McGraw-Edison, Westinghouse) and are already withheld from this edition. Figures 1-15 to 1-18 are block diagrams. Figures 1-2 to 1-5, 1-8 and 1-11 to 1-14 are eligible but simpler or overlapping.
8. VA Electrical Design Manual, March 2025 edition, hosted by VA at https://www.cfm.va.gov/til/dManual/dmElec-2025-03.pdf (`raw/va-edm2025`): its Appendix A carries revised versions of the same two topologies, embedded at about 66 dpi, too coarse to read; the 2019 edition (about 90 dpi) was used.

## Doubts and caveats

1. `va-edm2019`: the manual has no explicit public-domain or licence sentence; it is used as a VA publication (17 U.S.C. 105), and both drawings carry only the VA title block. If VA engaged a consultant to draft them, that is not stated anywhere.
2. `navfac-mo201`: an official NAVFAC publication with no explicit public-domain sentence. Third-party photographs are credited and withheld, and the one-line figures carry no credit, which supports treating them as the Navy's own; the generic distribution configurations do resemble those in industry handbooks, but no source is given.
3. The Army technical manuals say "prepared by or for the Government", so a contractor may have drafted them, but each declares itself public property and not subject to copyright except for the items it lists, and the figures used are not listed.
4. `ufc3-520-01-2002` and `ufc3-550-03fa` are cancelled editions; cancellation does not change their status as federal works. Their renders leave out the archive's CANCELLED stamp; the render settings of every sheet (page, crop, dpi, rotation, stamp removal) are in `manifest.json`, so a stamped version can be rendered from the raw file if wanted.
5. `tm5-693-p87` is a near-duplicate of the lower half of `tm5-694-p76` (same UPS switchboard, different rendering); keep it in mind when averaging.
6. WBDG (wbdg.org, run by the National Institute of Building Sciences) is not itself a federal host, but it is the distribution point the DoD names for Unified Facilities Criteria; the files are the agencies' documents.

## Rendering and tools

- pypdfium2 5.14.0 (licence BSD-3-Clause or Apache-2.0, PDFium under BSD-style terms), installed with pip into a scratch virtual environment, not the project or the global Python. Pillow 11.0.0 was already installed and was reused. PyMuPDF is present in the global Python on this machine but was not used; no GPL tool was used.
- Renders are 8-bit grey PNG at the dpi given per sheet; crops are fractions of the page; three TM 5-694 drawings are rotated 90 degrees clockwise so the text reads left to right. All rendering ran on the CPU in one process.
- Downloads were read only by PDFium (rendering and text extraction) and the `file` type check; the build scripts live outside the repository, in the session scratch folder.
